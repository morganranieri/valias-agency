Business and partner details
  Start date: 2026-September-01
  Purpose: Build and maintain Tutable - administrative software for extra-curricular education providers in Australia. 

Ownership and contributions
  Ownership: Each partner owns 50% of the business. 
  Contributions: Each partner will contribute $300 initial capital and their ongoing efforts, as needed. 

Profit and loss sharing
  Profit and loss sharing will be proportional to share ownership.   

Intellectual property (IP)
  Any software, code, documentation, trademarks, customer lists, and intellectual property developed for the business will belong to the partnership.

Making decisions 
  The following decisions should have unanimous agreement: 
    Entering into contractual debt greater than $100. 
    Making a purchase greater than $100. 
    Withdrawing any capital from the company. 
    Hiring any employee. 
    Permitting an employee to do any of these. 
    Selling any significant assets.
    Selling any IP.
    Publishing closed source IP, including code and version control history.

Dispute resolution
  Step one: decide a timeframe by when the matter should be settled. 
  Step two: clarify the issue by defining and agreeing on the terms of reference. 
  Step three: jointly appoint an arbiter. 
  Step four: present the issue to the arbiter, seeking to find a resolution. 

  If arbitration fails, the matter should be settled by a court of law. 

Adding or removing partners
  Adding: By unanimous agreement, a partner can be added.  
  Removing by choice: Provided no debts are outstanding, a partner can leave the partnership at any time.
  Removing by force: If a partner is imprisoned, they may be removed from the partnership. 
  Death: If a partner dies, their share is cancelled. 
  Bankruptcy: If a partner becomes bankrupt, their share is cancelled. 
  Permanent incapacity: If a partner becomes permanently incapacitated, their share is cancelled. 

Ending the partnership
  Last person standing: if only one partner remains, the partnership will be ended and remaining assets belong to the surviving partner.
  Mutual parting: if the partnership is dissolved by mutual agreement, any remaining assets should be divided equally.
